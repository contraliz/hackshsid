# HackSHSID 2026 course and event requirements

## Source-stated context

The course handout describes HackSHSID 2026 as part of BitDay, the Computer Science subject day. Participation is required for Grade 10 and Grade 11 Computer Science students, and the project is assessed as a semester project contributing to the term score. Teams may have one to three students, including students across classes or grades, and each team selects one track.

The project objectives are to identify user needs and translate a real problem into requirements; use computational thinking and programming to build a working prototype; test with evidence and evaluate strengths and limitations; develop teamwork, project management, and problem-solving; and use AI responsibly while explaining the team's own design decisions and contribution.

## Student/team deliverables and rules

The handout says teams must build a runnable prototype that demonstrates the main solution. Any suitable language, framework, or development tool may be used. AI tools are allowed, but teams must check their output, disclose use, and be able to explain submitted work. External code, libraries, datasets, and other sources must be acknowledged, with team work distinguished from reused or AI-generated material. Data must be public, synthetic, or appropriately authorized. Personal information, passwords, and API keys must not be published in a public repository.

Submission through SHSID Learn is expected by the event deadline and includes:

1. Group members.
2. Project inspiration and description, including how the problem was interpreted and approached and why the method is distinctive.
3. A public GitHub repository with source code, required files, and a README explaining installation, dependencies, running the project, data sources, and assumptions.
4. Testing evidence with representative cases, expected and actual results, and known limitations.
5. A brief AI-use statement describing tools, their contribution, and how the team checked or revised their output.

## Timeline and evaluation

The handout gives development/submission as 17–24 October 2026, preliminary review as 25 October 2026, and shortlisted presentations as 27–29 October during lunch in the Zhongxing Building; the exact submission time and updates are deferred to the event announcement. Shortlisted teams prepare a five-minute presentation/demo followed by three minutes of Q&A.

The rubric weights Innovation 30%, Technical complexity 25%, Impact 25%, Collaboration and contribution 10%, and Presentation 10%. Evidence asks for purposeful design and useful features; a working implementation and explanation of core components; user-needs evidence, meaningful results, and honest limitations; clear responsibilities and individual contributions; and a clear explanation/demo with accurate materials and answers demonstrating individual understanding.

Presentation materials should explain problem, design, implementation, results, and limitations, and include a live demonstration.

## Practical checklist derived from the handout

- Maintain a runnable prototype and reproducible setup instructions.
- Keep data provenance and assumptions visible.
- Preserve test cases with expected versus actual behavior and limitations.
- Record team ownership/contributions and AI use as the work develops.
- Prepare a concise demo and be ready to explain design choices independently.

These bullets are a practical synthesis of the handout, not a claim that the deliverables have been completed.

## Evidence distinction and unknowns

The dates, submission contents, permissions, team-size rule, and rubric weights above are stated by the course handout. The checklist is inferred as a way to operationalize those statements. The handout does not establish this team's members, selected track, repository URL, submission status, or any later announcement changes; verify those independently before relying on them.
